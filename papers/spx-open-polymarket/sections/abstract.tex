Polymarket's daily ``SPX Opens Up or Down'' market asks one question: does the
official S\&P 500 open --- the first index tick at 9:30 ET --- print above or
below the prior close? The crowd prices it as if the overnight futures gap
carries over in full ($k=1$, $\sigma=0$). It does not. Measured over 200
trading days, only about 80\% of the gap survives into the official open
($k \approx 0.77$--$0.80$). We trade that spread with a frozen linear model,
fitted once and never refit on the test half. Out of sample, accuracy is a
ladder, not a number: 72.5\% at midnight, rising to roughly 94\% at 9:29.
Standing down on coin-toss days --- midnight confidence below 65\% --- lifts
committed midnight accuracy to 82.1\%. A fee- and execution-audited backtest
leaves one survivor of 15 strategy candidates: entering at the first confident
hour turns \$100 into \$321.53 at flat \$50 stakes, 26 wins against 2 losses;
the hour-4 hold variant reaches \$135.92 staking 10\% of bankroll per trade.
The caveats stand: one market regime, 58 test days, a thinning market, a crowd
that can learn.
