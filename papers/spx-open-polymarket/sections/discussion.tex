\subsection{Anatomy of the misses}

Every miss has a name. Of the 55 midnight misses in 200 days, 33\% were
no-signal coin tosses and 51\% were genuine overnight news reversals:
flips at the Europe open, in the US pre-market, or in the 8:30 data
window. The remaining 16\% were auction quirks, including the
crowd-massacre days of Jan 29, Feb 24, Mar 10, and Apr 9. For v1.2's
committed calls the attribution is exhaustive: the 12 midnight misses are
12 news reversals (three flipped at the Europe open, six in the US
pre-market, three in the 8:30 window). The six misses at 7:00 are five
news days plus one quirk, Apr 9. Nothing is left over.

Figure~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_misses} shows what survives to 9:29 in
the minute-level sample: two misses in 37 days, one of each remaining
kind.

The misses also cluster. Eight of the 20 confident midnight misses came
from volatile March 2026 alone: a sigma calibrated on average conditions
turns nominal 85\% days into real 70\% days once the regime shifts. That
is the regime-dependent overnight risk \citet{ahoniemi2016overnight}
document, and the clustering, not any single miss, motivated the
regime-aware sigma in v1.2. The timing is just as lopsided: 24 of the 27
news-miss days had their last flip in the 8:30--9:30 window, after every
practical entry hour.

The sigma decomposition locates the model's boundary precisely. Pure
mapping and auction noise measure $\pm 0.08$--$0.09\%$, the 9:29 floor,
while the rest of the midnight $\pm 0.34\%$ is future news,
$\sqrt{0.34^2 - 0.09^2} \approx \pm 0.33\%$. About 94\% of midnight
uncertainty is events that have not happened yet. Therefore a perfect
per-stock model at midnight would add only one or two accuracy points.
The climb in Figure~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor} is mostly news
resolving, not the model improving.

\subsection{The two test losses}

Both test losses are fully attributed, and they are different kinds of
loss. Jul 2 was a news reversal: a 4:00 DOWN entry at 67.5 cents with an
up-probability of 0.19, then an 8:30 release that blew the gap to
$+0.27\%$. The model reached 0.97 only at 9:00, and by 8:29 the market
already stood at 0.835. About \$77 of the \$296 loss was recoverable at
8:29, and nothing later.

Jul 10 is the more instructive day. We had classified it as an unknowable
auction quirk. It was in fact a late futures flip, the gap moving from
$-0.03\%$ at 9:00 to $+0.08\%$ at 9:29 while the book lagged at
0.715/0.53. A sell-and-flip in the 8:29--9:28 window turns the $-\$307$
loss into $+\$347$, a \$654 swing, with futures data alone. That
reclassification is why the deployed rule carries position monitors and a
9:28 futures leg.

\subsection{Quirk days and the crowd}

The true quirk days are a different animal. Roughly once a month the
official print lands opposite to, or within a fraction of a point of, the
direction futures held all night. Futures cannot catch these days at any
hour; that is empirically confirmed, and v1.2 does not pretend to. It
keeps 4 of 12 quirk days safe, right or no-bet at 9:00, exactly as
the baseline does. What it improves is the confidence held on the wrong
side: Mar 10 from 84\% to 66\%, Apr 9 from 84\% to 70\%.

The residual is the auction mechanism itself, the modern descendant of
the stale-open effect \citep{stollwhaley1990}. Even at 9:29 the
futures error floor is $\pm 6$ index points; on Jun 22 (official $-0.14$
points) every model version said $+1.7$ to $+5.7$. Inside the floor, no
price data helps.

The crowd handles these days worst of all. On Mar 10 a bettor posted that
TradingView showed an open of 6{,}792.83 while the official open printed
6{,}796.56, on the other side of the prior close. The retail data feed
and the resolution source disagree exactly when it matters. Hedging with
ETFs or futures fails for the same reason: both track the wrong number,
and Jun 22 showed the ETF leg breaking around a dividend date. Comment
volume tells the rest: median 3 per day, 41 on Apr 9. On these days,
standing down is not a weakness. It is the only correct call futures data
can deliver.

\subsection{The ceiling and the replica path}

The 96.3\% ceiling is not a law of nature. It is the limit of
market-price data, and the one priced path past it is the auction
replica: assemble the official first tick from the exchanges' own
pre-open feeds instead of inferring it from futures. Nasdaq's NOII,
``the best predictor of the opening price available to the public''
\citep{nasdaq2026totalview}, runs at one-second cadence from 9:28 on
the post-2021 feed. Measured on sample days, it implies an index-level
replica error near 1.7 points at 9:29 and 0.75 at 9:29:50, against the
$\pm 6$-point futures floor: 3--8$\times$ sharper in the final minute.

The expected payoff was computed before spending anything. It moves 9:29
accuracy from roughly 94\% to 97--98\% and quirk days from 2 of 12
strictly called correctly on futures alone to a majority called. On the
tiniest quirk days the replica prices the abandoned 1--6-cent side at its
true 60--80\%: a several-fold expected-value trade even when the winner
is unknowable. These are projections from measured sample-day sharpness,
not backtest results.

There is no cheaper shortcut. A constituent replica built from pre-market
trades adds nothing over the futures gap, stale prints and regression
weights going to zero, so beating futures requires quotes and auction
data. One design question also stays open. Inverse-variance fusion
assumes independent voices, but the futures and replica voices are nested
and the news and gap voices are correlated; the Monte Carlo scenario
shape is discarded at the Gaussian boundary. Scenario-reweighting fusion
is the proposed fix. We state the problem and leave it unsolved.

\subsection{Who else is in the market}

The competition scan says the edge exists because the informed slots are
empty. The trade tape shows two serious automated actors. One is a print
sniper buying the winner at exactly four seconds after 9:30:00, at
0.989--0.999, in \$1--8k daily clips. The other is a mechanical longshot
harvester that took the cheap winning side on both recent quirk days:
about 1{,}000 trades in three weeks, median buy 15 cents, 93\% of buys
under 50 cents. The 9:25--9:29 buyers at 99 cents and above are
futures confirmers with no informational edge. Nobody shows
auction-informed behavior before the print. On Jun 30 the crowd sat at
35\% on a day that resolved Up, and even the sniper waited.

The insider scan is negative twice over. All 14 wallets with 100\%
day-win rates are stale-book harvesters: average entries at 66--79 cents
on days the model already priced at 0.60--0.72, with hard fills going
about 50\%. The best streak found, 12 of 12 days at a market-implied
joint probability of 2.4\%, is what chance produces among 242 wallets:
about six such streaks are expected. No pre-8:30 release-morning
accumulation appears anywhere, so no BLS-leak signature. The always-right
wallets are manually running our own early-entry stale-book edge at
\$200--8k scale, which validates it and shows the informed slots remain
unoccupied. Against the books directly, the model's Brier score is 0.059
to the crowd's 0.071, worth about 20\% per trade at the 5-cent threshold
over 18 indicative days.

Hindsight bounds what remains. A perfect oracle over the 58 test days
makes $+\$7{,}315$ against our $+\$164$. More than \$4{,}600 of it comes
from five late quirk-day collapses, and Jun 1 alone, the winning side
at 1.8 cents at 9:29, a 55$\times$, is 82\% of the entire ceiling. A
timing-only oracle on our own 21 days and sides makes $+\$569$, about
3.5$\times$ ours, and it is unharvestable by any tested rule. On 31 of 58
days the best price occurred between midnight and 2:00, exactly when the
model is least confident.

The regime is also moving. The train half's average win multiplier was
1.45 against the test half's 1.28, taker fees of \$0.09--1.02 per trade
arrived on Mar 30, and the profitable entry window has been drifting
earlier. Monthly re-validation is mandatory, not hygiene. Execution
shapes the rest. Our \$20--500 clips on a \$30--48k/day book are the
price impact, so the stale-book edge is structurally a maker strategy
\citep{polymarket2026orders}. A 5-cent modeled edge is not 5 cents of
capturable edge on a taker fill.

\subsection{Negative results}

The catalogue of dead ends is part of the result. On the model side, the
2--3am overnight drift is dead since 2021, so there is no drift term.
Tercile-$k$ without the widened regime sigma is worse than the baseline
it refines, and no single news gather-time works: the decisive moves
spread across the whole night.

The news layer earns its place only as a calendar: about 70\% of ES jumps
occur overnight, and every major US release lands at 8:30. The
deterministic overlay changes almost nothing at current settings (train
\$357 versus \$359, test \$314 versus \$322). The first LLM replay went 2
correct, 1 missed, and 3 wrong-signed out of 6: every wrong sign a
retrieval failure, not a reasoning failure. Retrieval quality dominates
model quality.

On the strategy side, Table~\ref{tab:strategy_graveyard} covers the
families; the designs that died before reaching it are more instructive.
Dip-hunting is refuted by adverse selection. On winning days the median
post-signal dip is 2 cents; on losing days it is 62 cents, the crash to
zero. Every patient variant loses to immediate taker entry on both
halves: dip-triggered entries, resting maker orders one to three cents
inside, 50/50 splits. After a valid signal the market converges
away from you.

Midnight entry is refuted by sizing. Deciding at 00:00 with \$50 stakes
went to ruin on train despite 62\% winners: a 72.5\%-accurate signal
cannot carry a stake. Hour 4 is the earliest hour where confidence
justifies money. Optimal sizing at a small bankroll is all-in by another
name; the compounding rule caps itself at half the balance for exactly
that reason.

\subsection{Limitations}

\paragraph{One regime, 58 test days.} The strategy evidence spans a single
market regime and 58 evaluation days, and the crowd on the other side can
learn. Every dollar figure is conditional on that window. More
walked-forward months are the only fix.

\paragraph{Midpoint price curves.} The backtest prices are CLOB midpoints,
not prints. Taker fills through the modeled spread are conservative, but
anything maker-side is untrustworthy at midpoint resolution, which is
why maker strategies were declared unverifiable rather than profitable.
Recording real fills in paper and live trading fixes this.

\paragraph{A shrinking market.} Volume fell from about \$664k per day in
January 2026 to \$30--48k by July 2026, and the 1\%-of-volume stake
ceiling falls one-for-one with it. There is no fix on our side; the number
to watch is daily volume.

\paragraph{The 465-day window is not validated.} Extending the fit window
to 465 days degrades late-hour calibration because the train half then
swallows the tariff-chaos regime. The 200-day numbers are the validated
reference, and a regime-aware fit is the prerequisite for trusting longer
windows.

\paragraph{The replica has never seen real data.} All replica machinery is
validated on synthetic self-tests only, and its measured inputs come from
a handful of free sample days that mostly do not cover the 2026 quirk
days. Running the replica on real NOII and imbalance days is the fix.

\paragraph{Execution unknowns.} Placement-to-fill latency, partial-fill
and nonce failure modes, the minimum order size, and websocket
fill-monitoring semantics on the live CLOB are unmeasured. Paper trading
against the live book answers each of them.

\paragraph{Research licenses do not cover production.} The affordable
auction data is academic: WRDS TAQ \citep{wrds2026taq} is
research-only, LSEG Tick History \citep{lseg2026tickhistory} is
research-only and IP-allowlisted, and the historical Nasdaq NOII gap is
metered \citep{databento2026equities}. Validation can stay free;
going live needs commercially licensed feeds, either a live broker feed at
roughly \$17--40 per month or commercial imbalance data
\citep{nyse2026taqimbalances}.

\paragraph{The macro calendar is thin.} BLS blocks scripted schedule
fetches, so without a FRED key the calendar reduces to the NFP
first-Friday rule and flags only 20 of 465 days. A FRED key or a manually
maintained release JSON restores the CPI, PPI, and GDP mornings.
