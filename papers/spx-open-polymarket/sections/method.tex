\subsection{Data and ground truth}
\label{sec:data}

Ground truth is the Yahoo \texttt{\^{}GSPC} daily open, cross-checked against
stooq \citep{yahoo2026chartapi,stooq2026spx}. Over 330 days the two
sources agree on every official open within 0.75 index points, which settled
an early scare: September 17 and December 3, 2025 were real auction-quirk
days, not data bugs. Days on which the sources disagree are listed by an
audit command and excluded from fits. All timestamps are ET, and gaps are
expressed in percent of the prior close.

Every input is free. Futures and index series (ES, NQ and YM futures,
\texttt{\^{}GSPC}, \texttt{\^{}VIX1D}) come from the Yahoo chart API; index
weights and venue tags from Slickcharts and Nasdaq's listed-securities file
\citep{slickcharts2026sp500,nasdaqtrader2026listed}; market odds from
Polymarket's gamma API \citep{polymarket2026gamma}; news from GDELT DOC
2.0 \citep{gdelt2017doc}; the macro release calendar from the FRED
release-dates API, with a keyless NFP first-Friday rule as fallback
\citep{fred2026releasedates}. Yahoo's history caps set the windows: a
720-day lookback (hourly bars stop near 730 days), a 480-day dataset window,
and only about 30 days of ES minute bars. The runtime is Python~3.12
standard library only, no pandas or numpy in core paths and no compiled
components, because the system is network-bound and makes one prediction
per day.

The train/test split is chronological halves. Nothing is ever fitted on the
test half.

\subsection{Core model}
\label{sec:core_model}

The day-level core is one linear map plus a clock:
\begin{equation}
\hat g = a_0 + k\,g, \qquad
P(\mathrm{up}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{a_0 + k\,g}{\sigma(\tau)}\right), \qquad
\sigma(\tau)^2 \approx 0.0066 + 0.0002\,\tau .
\label{eq:core}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$g$ & overnight E-mini futures gap, in \% of prior close \\
$\hat g$ & predicted official-open gap, same units \\
$a_0$ & fitted intercept, $0.0092\%$ \\
$k$ & gap pass-through, pooled $0.767$--$0.797$ (default $0.767$) \\
$\tau$ & minutes remaining before 9:30~ET \\
$\sigma(\tau)$ & prediction width in \% units, fitted intraday \\
$\Phi$ & standard normal CDF \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The fitted $k$ says the official open realizes only about 80\% of the
futures gap. The width follows the fitted schedule and shrinks toward the
open; measured hourly it runs $\pm0.344\%$ at midnight, $\pm0.30\%$ at
04:00, $\pm0.15\%$ at 09:00 and $\pm0.083\%$ at 9:29. Every constant was
fitted once on the train half and frozen.

Open positions also carry a flip risk, the chance that the effective gap
changes sign before 9:30; Appendix~\ref{app:execution} gives that model.

\subsection{Regime and news extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

Model v1.2, the final day-level fit, keeps Equation~\ref{eq:core} and
conditions its constants. The pass-through becomes tercile-$k$: 0.733,
0.827 and 0.839 for the calm, mid and volatile trailing-volatility
terciles, so spillover rises with volatility, matching the post-Lehman
pattern of \citet{ahoniemi2016overnight}. Gaps under the $0.25\%$
small-gap threshold carry nearly one-for-one ($k \approx 1.0$) through hour
1, decaying over $0.68$--$0.90$ across hours 1--4, consistent with
reported large-gap reversal effects, and all gap sizes converge to
$k \approx 0.78$--$0.80$ by 7:00. A regime multiplier widens $\sigma$ from
a no-lookahead blend of the EWMA of squared 9:00 gaps
($\lambda = 0.94$) and the prior day's ES realized variance. On NFP
mornings $\sigma$ is multiplied by 1.29 before 8:30, fitted on the 11
training NFP mornings with clamp bounds $(1.0, 2.5)$.

Model v1.3 adds \texttt{\^{}VIX1D} to the $\sigma$ blend and an NQ--ES
spread term. Production runs v1.3 before 5:00 and v1.2 from 5:00 on. One
guardrail is deliberate: no end-to-end machine learning on roughly 500
day-level labels, which would overfit and destroy calibration. The
day-level core stays linear; learned models are reserved for the per-stock
replica sample, about two million (stock, second) rows once real auction
data lands.

Appendix~\ref{app:news} details the news layer, its design rules and
schedule, the two voice groups, and the registry of every fitted constant
with its fitting window (Table~\ref{tab:fitted_constants}).

\subsection{Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

The futures core, the news groups, and later the replica combine by
inverse-variance pooling:
\begin{equation}
\mu_F = \frac{\sum_i \mu_i / \sigma_i^2}{\sum_i 1/\sigma_i^2}, \qquad
\frac{1}{\sigma_F^2} = \sum_i \frac{1}{\sigma_i^2}, \qquad
P(\mathrm{up}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_F}{\sigma_F}\right).
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$\mu_i, \sigma_i$ & voice $i$'s gap estimate and its uncertainty \\
$\mu_F, \sigma_F$ & fused estimate and uncertainty \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

News voices carry floors on $\sigma_i$, so they nudge the fused estimate
but never outvote the market voice. The sharpest voice dominates
automatically. The replica voice, which assembles the official first tick
from per-stock auction previews instead of futures, is specified in
Appendix~\ref{app:replica}.

\subsection{Strategy laboratory}
\label{sec:strategy_lab}

The laboratory replays every candidate strategy against all 120 resolved
markets since the series began on January 22, 2026, fetched with full
minute-level price curves; 115 of them join to the model's hourly
probabilities. The model was fitted only on pre-2026 data, so every
strategy day is out of sample. The split is chronological: 57 train days
through April 15, then 58 test days from April 16 to July 14. The curves
are CLOB midpoints, not prints \citep{polymarket2026clob}, so every
strategy carries a volume gate.

Execution is modeled, not assumed: Appendix~\ref{app:execution} gives the
fee model, the calibrated spread and impact, the pessimistic maker-fill
rule, and the four audit defects fixed before any conclusion was drawn.

Candidates read the model through one edge module:
\begin{equation}
c = \max(p_m,\, 1 - p_m), \qquad
e_{\mathrm{up}} = p_m - q, \qquad
e_{\mathrm{down}} = q - p_m.
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$p_m$ & model probability of an up open \\
$q$ & market price of the UP side, in USDC \\
$c$ & model confidence \\
$e_{\mathrm{up}}, e_{\mathrm{down}}$ & edge of buying the UP or DOWN side \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

A side is bought only when its edge reaches the 0.05 minimum price
divergence; below $c = 0.65$ the module answers coin-flip zone, stand
down. The deployed rule is seven steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at 00:05, run the Group-A briefing;
  \item re-fuse the voices at every checkpoint and event trigger;
  \item enter at the first hour in 4--9 with edge $\ge 5$ cents,
    traded-side probability $\ge 0.70$ and price $\le 95$ cents, as an
    immediate taker;
  \item stake $\min(\mbox{half balance}, \$400, 1\%\ \mbox{of day
    volume})$, and skip days below \$1k volume;
  \item at 08:35 and 9:00, run position monitors: sell if the traded
    side's fused probability drops below 0.5, flip if the other side
    fully qualifies;
  \item at 9:28, re-check against live futures: enter remaining edges
    $\ge 3$ cents, sell-and-flip on contradiction (the replica replaces
    this voice when live);
  \item at 9:30, log fills against the resolution and refit monthly,
    walk-forward.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Selection correction.}
The laboratory walked 15 unique verified candidates forward, and the
deployed rule is the family's maximum, so its test record is scored with
White's reality check \citep{white2000reality}:
\begin{equation}
V = \max_{k \le K} \sqrt{n}\,\bar f_k, \qquad
p_{\mathrm{adj}} = \frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
\mathbf{1}\!\left[\max_{k \le K} \sqrt{n}
\left(\bar f^{\,*b}_k - \bar f_k\right) \ge V\right].
\label{eq:reality_check}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$\bar f_k$ & candidate $k$'s mean daily test PnL at flat \$100 stakes \\
$n$ & test days, 58 \\
$K$ & family size: 15 recorded, 17 with the later-selected variants \\
$\bar f^{\,*b}_k$ & the same mean on bootstrap resample $b$ \\
$B$ & bootstrap draws, 10{,}000 \\
$V$ & the best candidate's scaled performance \\
$p_{\mathrm{adj}}$ & chance that a no-edge family produces a best this good \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Days are resampled jointly across candidates with a stationary bootstrap
\citep{politisromano1994}, mean block length five days, which preserves
cross-candidate correlation and short-range autocorrelation; each candidate
is centered at its own mean, White's least-favorable null. A binomial test
on the win--loss record was rejected on purpose: entries average 80--90
cents, so a no-edge bettor also wins most trades, and mean PnL is the
honest statistic. The correction is a lower bound, because train-side grid
searches touched over 100 parameter cells and only the walked-forward
candidates are recorded.
