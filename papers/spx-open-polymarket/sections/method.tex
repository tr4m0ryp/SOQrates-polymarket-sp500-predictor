\subsection{Data and ground truth}
\label{sec:data}

Ground truth is the Yahoo \texttt{\^{}GSPC} daily open, cross-checked against
stooq \citep{yahoo2026chartapi,stooq2026spx}. Over 330 days the two
sources agree on every official open within 0.75 index points, which settled
an early scare: September 17 and December 3, 2025 were real auction-quirk
days, not data bugs. Days on which the sources disagree are listed by an
audit command and excluded from fits. All timestamps are ET, and gaps are
expressed in percent of the prior close.

Every input is free. Futures and index series (ES, NQ and YM futures,
\texttt{\^{}GSPC}, \texttt{\^{}VIX1D}) come from the Yahoo chart API; index
weights and venue tags from Slickcharts and Nasdaq's listed-securities file
\citep{slickcharts2026sp500,nasdaqtrader2026listed}; market odds from
Polymarket's gamma API \citep{polymarket2026gamma}; news from GDELT DOC
2.0 \citep{gdelt2017doc}; the macro release calendar from the FRED
release-dates API, with a keyless NFP first-Friday rule as fallback
\citep{fred2026releasedates}. Yahoo's history caps set the windows: a
720-day lookback (hourly bars stop near 730 days), a 480-day dataset window,
and only about 30 days of ES minute bars. The runtime is Python~3.12
standard library only, no pandas or numpy in core paths and no compiled
components, because the system is network-bound and makes one prediction
per day.

The train/test split is chronological halves. Nothing is ever fitted on the
test half.

\subsection{Core model}
\label{sec:core_model}

The day-level core is one linear map plus a clock:
\begin{equation}
\hat g = a_0 + k\,g, \qquad
P(\mathrm{up}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{a_0 + k\,g}{\sigma(\tau)}\right), \qquad
\sigma(\tau)^2 \approx 0.0066 + 0.0002\,\tau .
\label{eq:core}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$g$ & overnight E-mini futures gap, in \% of prior close \\
$\hat g$ & predicted official-open gap, same units \\
$a_0$ & fitted intercept, $0.0092\%$ \\
$k$ & gap pass-through, pooled $0.767$--$0.797$ (default $0.767$) \\
$\tau$ & minutes remaining before 9:30~ET \\
$\sigma(\tau)$ & prediction width in \% units, fitted intraday \\
$\Phi$ & standard normal CDF \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The fitted $k$ says the official open realizes only about 80\% of the
futures gap. The width follows the fitted schedule and shrinks toward the
open; measured hourly it runs $\pm0.344\%$ at midnight, $\pm0.30\%$ at
04:00, $\pm0.15\%$ at 09:00 and $\pm0.083\%$ at 9:29. Every constant was
fitted once on the train half and frozen.

For open positions we also score the risk that the effective gap changes
sign before 9:30. The center drifts about $0.014\%$ per square-root minute,
so
\begin{equation}
P(\mathrm{flip}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{-\,\lvert 0.83\,g\rvert}
{0.014\%\,\sqrt{\tau}}\right).
\label{eq:flip}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$g$ & current futures gap, in \% of prior close \\
$0.83$ & effective pass-through used in the drift model \\
$0.014\%$ & fitted drift of the center per $\sqrt{\text{minute}}$ \\
$\tau$ & minutes remaining before 9:30~ET \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The scale matters more than the formula. A $+0.40\%$ gap at 7:00 carries
about 1\% flip risk; a $+0.10\%$ gap at the same hour carries about 30\%.

\subsection{Regime and news extensions}
\label{sec:extensions}

Model v1.2, the final day-level fit, keeps Equation~\ref{eq:core} and
conditions its constants. The pass-through becomes tercile-$k$: 0.733,
0.827 and 0.839 for the calm, mid and volatile trailing-volatility
terciles, so spillover rises with volatility, matching the post-Lehman
pattern of \citet{ahoniemi2016overnight}. Gaps under the $0.25\%$
small-gap threshold carry nearly one-for-one ($k \approx 1.0$) through hour
1, decaying over $0.68$--$0.90$ across hours 1--4, consistent with
reported large-gap reversal effects, and all gap sizes converge to
$k \approx 0.78$--$0.80$ by 7:00. A regime multiplier widens $\sigma$ from
a no-lookahead blend of the EWMA of squared 9:00 gaps
($\lambda = 0.94$) and the prior day's ES realized variance. On NFP
mornings $\sigma$ is multiplied by 1.29 before 8:30, fitted on the 11
training NFP mornings with clamp bounds $(1.0, 2.5)$.

Model v1.3 adds \texttt{\^{}VIX1D} to the $\sigma$ blend and an NQ--ES
spread term. Production runs v1.3 before 5:00 and v1.2 from 5:00 on. One
guardrail is deliberate: no end-to-end machine learning on roughly 500
day-level labels, which would overfit and destroy calibration. The
day-level core stays linear; learned models are reserved for the per-stock
replica sample, about two million (stock, second) rows once real auction
data lands.

\paragraph{News design rules.}
On our own event days, news publication and market reaction are
simultaneous within one 15-minute GDELT indexing cycle: the March 23 Iran
story was first indexed at 12:00 UTC, the same hour ES swung $+2\%$. Two
rules follow. The LLM can never front-run headlines, and the LLM never
sees market data: market moves only trigger runs.

\paragraph{Schedule.}
Checkpoints run at 02:30, 04:30, 07:00, 08:00, 08:35 (mandatory,
post-release) and 09:15, plus an event trigger on any $0.15\%$ gap move
within 15 minutes; LLM runs are capped at $6+6$ per night. The calendar
itself is thin: BLS blocks scripted schedule fetches, so without a FRED
key only the NFP first-Friday rule applies, flagging 20 of 465 days.

\paragraph{Group A: midnight briefing.}
At 00:05 an LLM briefing reads the calendar, earnings, nowcast-versus-
consensus and prediction-market structural odds; measured over 30 days,
structural odds move only about 0.2 points overnight, so one snapshot
stays fresh. Output is a variance multiplier $m_A \in [1, 2.5]$ anchored
at the fitted 1.29, and a directional tilt $\lvert d_A \rvert \le 0.3$
allowed only on nowcast--consensus disagreement. The primary model is
GitHub Models gpt-4.1, about 12 calls per night with 1--2K-token payloads.

\paragraph{Group B: fitted effects.}
Two deterministic effects are fitted from data. After a gap move of at
least $0.35\%$ in one hour within the last two hours, the official-open
residual is wider: pooled ratio 1.90 over 76 shock days, with unstable
halves. On conflicted days, where a shock happened but the gap sits under
$0.15\%$, the shock's sign predicts the official open 85.4\% of the
time ($n = 41$), encoded as $\mu_B = 0.9\,d_B\,c\,\sigma_m$ with the 0.9
chosen conservatively; the term is zero by construction on already-repriced
days. For the strategy backtest these constants were refit train-only
(shock 1.32, conflict 0.85) after the audit described below caught
pooled-fit look-ahead.

\subsection{Fusion}
\label{sec:fusion}

The futures core, the news groups, and later the replica combine by
inverse-variance pooling:
\begin{equation}
\mu_F = \frac{\sum_i \mu_i / \sigma_i^2}{\sum_i 1/\sigma_i^2}, \qquad
\frac{1}{\sigma_F^2} = \sum_i \frac{1}{\sigma_i^2}, \qquad
P(\mathrm{up}) = \Phi\!\left(\frac{\mu_F}{\sigma_F}\right).
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$\mu_i, \sigma_i$ & voice $i$'s gap estimate and its uncertainty \\
$\mu_F, \sigma_F$ & fused estimate and uncertainty \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

News voices carry floors on $\sigma_i$, so they nudge the fused estimate
but never outvote the market voice. The sharpest voice dominates
automatically.

\subsection{Auction replica}
\label{sec:replica}

The replica assembles the first tick from per-stock auction previews
instead of futures:
\begin{equation}
\mu_R = \sum_i w_i \left( \frac{\hat P_i}{C_i} - 1 \right)
\mathbf{1}[\,i \in \mbox{first tick}\,].
\label{eq:replica}
\end{equation}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{ll}
\toprule
Symbol & Meaning \\
\midrule
$w_i$ & stock $i$'s prior-close capitalization weight \\
$\hat P_i$ & predicted first print of stock $i$ \\
$C_i$ & stock $i$'s prior close \\
$\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ & one when stock $i$ opens in time for the first tick \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Stale stocks contribute zero, which keeps the whole computation in
divisor-free weighted-gap space. A Monte Carlo layer treats the first tick
as a random variable: certain-live names are pooled analytically,
certain-stale names sit at zero, and uncertain names are simulated under a
common slowness factor (a logit shift, $\beta = 1.0$) with per-stock
preview noise of $0.05\%$ for Nasdaq and $0.30\%$ for NYSE names, both
measured. The simulation outputs mean, sigma, $P(\mathrm{up})$, quantiles,
and the pivotal names, those whose inclusion swings $P(\mathrm{up})$
most, scored as $P(\mathrm{up} \mid \mathrm{in}) - P(\mathrm{up} \mid
\mathrm{out})$. A print filter conditions on crosses observed after
9:30:00, and the seed is fixed. Print timing uses venue priors, Nasdaq
$p_{\mathrm{live}} \approx 0.97$ and NYSE $\approx 0.15$, until real cross
timestamps allow a per-stock fit, with a Bayes update for names still
silent.

Inputs are Nasdaq's Net Order Imbalance Indicator and NYSE order
imbalances \citep{nasdaq2026totalview,nyse2026taqimbalances}. The ITCH
parser streams a raw $\sim$3.5~GB gzipped TotalView-ITCH day file and
finishes a full day in 27 seconds. Development ran on free full-day
samples: seven ITCH days from 2019--2020 \citep{nasdaq2026itchsamples}
and NYSE TAQ imbalance samples including April 1--2, 2026
\citep{nyse2026taqimbalances}. Those dates mostly miss the 2026 quirk
days, so they validate parsers, not the days that matter. The replica
machinery is validated on synthetic data only.

\subsection{Strategy laboratory}
\label{sec:strategy_lab}

The laboratory replays every candidate strategy against all 120 resolved
markets since the series began on January 22, 2026, fetched with full
minute-level price curves; 115 of them join to the model's hourly
probabilities. The model was fitted only on pre-2026 data, so every
strategy day is out of sample. The split is chronological: 57 train days
through April 15, then 58 test days from April 16 to July 14. The curves
are CLOB midpoints, not prints \citep{polymarket2026clob}, so every
strategy carries a volume gate.

Execution is modeled, not assumed. Polymarket's Fee Structure V2,
effective March 30, 2026, makes makers free with a 25\% rebate and charges
takers $\mathrm{shares} \times \mathrm{feeRate} \times p\,(1-p)$ with
feeRate 0.04 in the Finance category \citep{polymarket2026orders},
about \$1 per 100 shares near even odds. The entire test half is fee-era,
a built-in regime stress. From roughly 3,400 real trade prints across six
days we calibrated an effective half-spread of about 1 cent and a price
impact of about 0.05 cents per \$100 of stake; the binding constraint is
depth, not slope. Maker fills are modeled pessimistically: the price must
trade through the limit.

An independent audit of the harness found four defects, and all four were
fixed before any conclusion was drawn: fictional fills on zero-volume days
(one such fill carried 46\% of an early result's profit), an entry gate
that admitted bets against the model's own view, over-optimistic maker
fills, and look-ahead in the news-overlay constants, which were refit on
disjoint data. In a thin market, execution assumptions are as load-bearing
as the signal.

Candidates read the model through one edge module: minimum price
divergence 0.05, and below 65\% confidence the module answers coin-flip
zone, stand down. The deployed rule is seven steps:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item at 00:05, run the Group-A briefing;
  \item re-fuse the voices at every checkpoint and event trigger;
  \item enter at the first hour in 4--9 with edge $\ge 5$ cents,
    traded-side probability $\ge 0.70$ and price $\le 95$ cents, as an
    immediate taker;
  \item stake $\min(\mbox{half balance}, \$400, 1\%\ \mbox{of day
    volume})$, and skip days below \$1k volume;
  \item at 08:35 and 9:00, run position monitors: sell if the traded
    side's fused probability drops below 0.5, flip if the other side
    fully qualifies;
  \item at 9:28, re-check against live futures: enter remaining edges
    $\ge 3$ cents, sell-and-flip on contradiction (the replica replaces
    this voice when live);
  \item at 9:30, log fills against the resolution and refit monthly,
    walk-forward.
\end{enumerate}
