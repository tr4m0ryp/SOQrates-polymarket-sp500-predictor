Out-of-sample direction accuracy over the 200-day window (September 2025 to
July 2026) is a ladder, not a number. Table~\ref{tab:accuracy_ladder} reports
it by prediction hour: 72.5\% at midnight, rising through 77.0\% at 04:00 to
roughly 94\% at 9:29 on the minute-level sample, against a ceiling of 96.3\%
with perfect 9:30 market data. The rest is auction noise that no futures
price contains. Figure~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor} shows the same climb as
per-day probability trajectories.
Figures~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0000_200d}
and~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0400_200d} fix the call at midnight and at
04:00: 145 against 154 correct days, so four extra hours of futures data buy
4.5 accuracy points.

Not betting is part of the model. Dropping the days whose midnight confidence
sits below 65\% removes 88 of 200 days (44\%) and lifts midnight accuracy
from 72.5\% to 82.1\% (92 of 112), as
Figure~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0000_confident} shows. The filter is not a
reporting trick; it is the trading rule, and below 65\% the system stands
down.

\paragraph{Calibration.}
Table~\ref{tab:calibration} compares model versions on the test half
($n=100$ days). The gains are calibration, not direction: the intermediate
v1.1 already left direction accuracy unchanged while trimming the midnight
Brier score (mean squared probability error) from 0.1785 to 0.1751. v1.2 cuts
overconfident misses, calls wrong at $\geq$80\% confidence, from 6/6/3 at
hours 0/4/9 to 3/3/0, and committed accuracy on days at or above the 65\%
gate rises from 80.9\% to 81.5\%; its coin-flip share rises too, honest
reclassification of days the model should not trade rather than a defect.
Tercile-$k$ without the widened regime sigma is worse than baseline (7
overconfident misses), and the fitted release-morning multiplier of 1.29 is
small but right-signed (NFP-subset Brier 0.1993 to 0.1971). v1.3 sharpens the
early hours but is slightly worse late, so production runs v1.3 before 5:00
and v1.2 after. Quirk days stay beyond futures data: v1.2 keeps 4 of 12
safe, right or no-bet at 9:00 and the same as baseline, but softens wrong-side
confidence (Mar 10 from 84\% to 66\%, Apr 9 from 84\% to 70\%), and at 9:00
no wrong call at 80\% confidence or above remains. Extending the fit window
to 465 days degrades late-hour calibration because the train half then
swallows the tariff-chaos regime; the 200-day numbers remain the validated
reference.

\paragraph{Auction-feed sharpness.}
The auction feeds were measured on free sample days. On the one full ITCH
day (December 30, 2019; 3{,}086 Nasdaq symbols), the NOII, Nasdaq's
published indicative opening price, deviates from the official open by a
mean 10.4 basis points at 9:29 and 8.3 at 9:29:50, and by 4.7 and 3.2 when
weighted by the top-100 notional names. At index level that is a replica
error near 1.7 points at 9:29 and 0.75 at 9:29:50, against a futures floor
of roughly 6 points: 3--8$\times$ sharper in the final minute. The NYSE side
is far noisier: on the TAQ sample day (April 1, 2026; 21 NYSE mega-caps) the
median indicative deviation falls from 100 basis points at 9:00 to 43 at
9:29 and 29 at 9:29:50. That is 6--10$\times$ noisier than Nasdaq NOII,
which validates carrying NYSE names at prior close in the replica. The replica
machinery passes its self-tests (Monte Carlo matches the analytic case; a
synthetic quirk correctly reports JPM swinging $P(\text{up})$ by $-0.16$),
but it has never seen real auction data.

\paragraph{Strategy backtest.}
Of 15 verified strategy candidates, exactly one survives out of sample.
Table~\ref{tab:strategy_graveyard} lists the graveyard. The survivor enters
at 04:00~ET when the edge of Equation~\ref{eq:edge} is at least 5 cents
and the traded side's probability is at least 0.60, takes the taker fill,
and holds to resolution. Staking 10\% of bankroll per trade it turns \$100
into \$135.92 on the test half (\$131.83 with all execution costs doubled),
21 trades, 19W--2L, never dipping below the \$100 start; at flat \$50 stakes
the same rule reaches \$264.30, peak \$292.
Figure~\ref{fig:strategy_bankroll_50} shows both \$50-stake trajectories.

and under it the same 28 trades run \$100 to \$850.69 (\$669.44
stressed), peak \$1{,}307.76, maximum drawdown \$477.38; capping stakes at
\$1{,}000 instead reaches only \$901.02, because the market's own 1\%
ceiling binds first. The 0.70 gate is load-bearing: at 0.60 the late slice
goes to ruin on train, and late entries at 86--95 cents need roughly a 90\%
win rate just to break even. Entries above 95 cents are rejected outright,
while tightening that cap to 90 cents overshoots and deletes \$27 of
profitable low-90s days.

Sizing carries its own risk. A losses-first reordering of the flat-\$50 test
trades busts on day two ($-\$0.38$), while the guarded variant
$\min(\$50, 20\%\ \text{of bankroll})$ survives it, ending at \$176 with a
\$64 floor. The half-balance rule replayed on the train half, which holds
five losses including the quirk days Jan 29 and Feb 24, ends at \$211.99 but
touches \$96.53 on the way; losses-first on the test half survives but
touches \$24. Figure~\ref{fig:stake_sweep} sweeps flat stakes to \$2{,}000:
ROI per dollar is flat at 15.5--16\% from \$25 to \$300, the simulation
leaves its validated calibration region above roughly \$400, and doubling
the intended stake from \$1{,}000 to \$2{,}000 adds only about 27\% more
profit.

The rest of the edge sits in small, verified additions. Position monitors at
08:35 and 9:00, selling if the traded side's fused probability drops below
0.5 and flipping if the other side fully qualifies, added \$43 on train and edge
the champion on test, \$323.67 against \$321.53. A 9:28--9:29 live-futures
leg called the official open 13 of 13 on the only 13 days with minute-level
history and made $+\$107.35$ on three \$50 entries; it cannot be backtested
further and runs paper-first, and the true auction quirks (Jun 1, Jun 22)
stay invisible to it. The strategy is also mostly not trading: it sat out 37
of 58 test days, 30 of which never reach 70\% confidence with a 5-cent edge
at any hour, and trading those days anyway at a 0.60 gate went to ruin. Most
entries are 72--95-cent favorites paying \$4--25 per win; the signature
trade is Jul 14, UP at 49 cents, $+\$50.92$ on a \$50 stake, which alone
paid for both losses.
