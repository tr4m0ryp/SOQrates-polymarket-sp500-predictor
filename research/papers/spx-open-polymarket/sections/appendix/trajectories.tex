\subsection{Reliability of the 9:00 probabilities}
\label{app:reliability}

Figure~\ref{fig:reliability_diagram} tests the late-hour probabilities bin
by bin. At the 9:00 prediction time the production hand-off (v1.2 at that
hour) scores a test-half Brier of 0.0696 on the rebuilt cache, beside 0.0667
for the baseline and 0.0690 for v1.3 on the same split. The distribution is
bimodal: 24 of the 100 days sit below predicted 0.1 and all opened down, 24
sit above 0.9 and all opened up, and the thin middle bins (3--10 days each)
carry Wilson intervals that straddle the diagonal. No miscalibration is
detectable at this sample size; with 100 days that is a statement about the
sample as much as about the model.

\begin{figure}[htp]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.92\linewidth]{figures/reliability_diagram.pdf}
    \caption{Reliability diagram for the production model at the 9:00 ET
    prediction time, held-out test half of the validated 200-day window
    ($n=100$ days, 2026-02-12 to 2026-07-13; fitted on the first 100 days).
    Top: predicted $P(\text{up})$ binned into deciles against the observed
    frequency of up opens, with 95\% Wilson intervals per bin; the dashed
    diagonal is perfect calibration. Bottom: days per bin. The distribution
    is bimodal (24 days below 0.1, 24 above 0.9), so most 9:00 calls are
    near-certain and correct: 0 of 34 up opens in the two lowest bins, 34 of
    34 in the two highest. Brier score 0.0696. The 9:29 prediction time
    cannot be evaluated this way, because its minute-level history exists
    only as an unretained 18-day sample.}
    \label{fig:reliability_diagram}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Miss clustering and per-day trajectories}
\label{app:trajectory_figs}

The misses cluster. Eight of the 20 confident midnight misses came
from volatile March 2026 alone: a sigma calibrated on average conditions
turns nominal 85\% days into real 70\% days once the regime shifts. That
is the regime-dependent overnight risk \citet{ahoniemi2016overnight}
document, and the clustering, not any single miss, motivated the
regime-aware sigma in v1.2. The timing is just as lopsided: 24 of the 27
news-miss days had their last flip in the 8:30--9:30 window, after every
practical entry hour.

Figures~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0000_200d},
\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0400_200d}
and~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_0000_confident} show the per-day
probability trajectories behind the accuracy ladder of
Table~\ref{tab:accuracy_ladder};
Figure~\ref{fig:spx_open_predictor_misses} isolates the two 9:29 misses
attributed in Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.
